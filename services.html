02

The experiences people fly here for.

Each of these is a permit or a booked activity that slots into any routing. Numbers are set by the Uganda Wildlife Authority, not by us, and they are small.

Gorilla habituation Rushaga · 4 permits a day

Four hours with a family still being habituated, alongside the researchers and trackers. Long days, hard walking, and the closest thing to fieldwork a visitor can buy.

Chimpanzee habituation Kibale · from 05:30

Meet the trackers at the nest before the community wakes and stay with them until they build again. Six people maximum, and you will be wet by nine.

Shoebill and papyrus birding Mabamba Bay · Ramsar site

Out by motorised canoe at first light with a Mabamba Wetland Eco-Tourism Association guide. Shoebill, blue swallow, and papyrus gonolek in the same morning.

White-water on the Nile Jinja · grade III to V

A full day on the big water below the source with a safety kayaker per raft, or the family float on the grade III section for anyone under sixteen.

04

How a day with us actually runs.

The same four things happen on every trip, in the same order, whatever the routing says.

01

Coffee and the plan

Your guide runs through the day at breakfast: departure, distance, where lunch is and what time you will be off the road. Nothing is a surprise at four in the afternoon.

02

Roof up, radio on

Pop-top open in the parks, hatch closed on the transfers. Guides share sightings on the network, which is why we tend to reach the lions before the convoy does.

03

Off the road by six

Ugandan dusk is fast and Ugandan trucks do not have lights. We schedule arrivals before sunset and will change the plan rather than drive into the dark.

04

Tomorrow, confirmed

Permits, boats and ranger allocations for the next day are confirmed by the office each evening and the guide tells you before you go up.

In the vehicle

  • Toyota Land Cruiser extended, pop-top roof, seven seats used by six guests so everyone has a window
  • Cool box restocked daily with bottled water, and a refill jerrycan so nobody buys plastic in the parks
  • Charging at every seat, inverter for camera batteries, and a spare 4G router
  • Recovery kit, two spares, high-lift jack and a winch on the Bwindi and Rwenzori vehicles

With the guide

  • UWA-certified driver-guides, all with the bird and mammal papers, none subcontracted
  • Reference library in the vehicle: Stevenson and Fanshawe, Kingdon, and a Rwenzori flora
  • Binoculars for each guest, 8x42, cleaned between trips
  • First aid and a satellite messenger on every departure that leaves tarmac
